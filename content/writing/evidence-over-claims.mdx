## The problem with a conventional skills list

A portfolio can say **Python**, **AI Engineering**, or **Security Engineering**, but those labels alone do not explain what was actually built, how it was tested, or how confident a reviewer should be in the claim.

This project uses a simple principle:

> Professional capability should be supported by inspectable evidence wherever practical.

## The evidence chain

For this portfolio, useful evidence can come from several places:

- a working project or implementation;
- verification and validation artifacts;
- requirements and architecture decisions;
- security analysis and threat modelling;
- technical writing that explains decisions and lessons learned;
- formal education or certifications.

The goal is not to make every claim look equally mature. The goal is to make the current level of evidence visible.

## Capability maturity

The portfolio uses the following progression:

```text
Claimed -> Studied -> Implemented -> Tested -> Demonstrated
```

A capability can therefore grow as stronger evidence becomes available instead of being represented as a binary skill/no-skill label.

## Why this matters

This structure makes the portfolio easier to inspect and harder to inflate. It also creates a useful foundation for later features such as evidence-grounded portfolio search, job-description comparison, and a constrained portfolio assistant.

Those later systems should reason over structured evidence rather than inventing professional claims.
